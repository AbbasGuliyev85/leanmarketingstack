An email list is one of the few marketing assets you truly own. Search rankings shift and social platforms change their algorithms, but subscribers who opted in to hear from you stay reachable. That's why "start your email list early" is some of the most common advice for new bloggers.

The next question is which email platform to use. Three names come up constantly for beginners: **MailerLite**, **GetResponse** and **Kit** (formerly **ConvertKit**). This guide compares them honestly, without inventing feature lists. Email tools change plans and pricing frequently, so we don't print prices. Always confirm current details on the vendor's own site before you buy.

## The short version

- **MailerLite:** simple, clean and budget-friendly. A great default for most new bloggers.
- **Kit:** built specifically for creators (bloggers, YouTubers, course makers), with a strong focus on tagging subscribers and selling digital products.
- **GetResponse:** a broader marketing platform that bundles email with extra tools like webinars and landing pages. Good if you want more in one place.

## What every beginner actually needs

Before comparing, here's the honest list of what you'll need in your first year:

1. **A signup form or landing page** to collect emails.
2. **A welcome email or short welcome sequence** (automation).
3. **The ability to send a newsletter** when you publish something new.
4. **Basic segmentation**, such as tagging people by interest.
5. **Good deliverability**, so your emails reach inboxes.
6. **A free or cheap plan** while your list is small.

All three platforms cover the first five. Item six depends on current free-plan terms, which change, so check them.

## MailerLite

**What it's known for:** a clean, uncluttered interface and a strong reputation for value. Many beginners find it the easiest of the three to learn.

**Strengths:**
- Simple drag-and-drop email editor.
- Signup forms, landing pages and automations included.
- Generally considered good value as your list grows.

**Trade-offs:**
- Fewer advanced creator-commerce features than Kit.
- Fewer bundled extras (like webinars) than GetResponse.
- Account approval: some providers, including MailerLite, may review new accounts before letting them send, so set it up a few days before you need it.

**Best for:** new bloggers who want something simple and affordable that won't get in the way.

## How to choose in three questions

**1. Will you sell your own digital products within a year?**
If yes, look closely at **Kit**. If you don't know, don't let it drive the decision, because you can migrate later.

**2. Do you want webinars or funnels in the same tool?**
If yes, **GetResponse** is worth a look. If not, you'd be paying for features you won't use.

**3. Do you just want to collect emails and send good newsletters, cheaply?**
Then **MailerLite** is usually the simplest choice.

## A realistic first-90-days email plan

Whichever tool you pick, here's a plan that works for new blogs:

1. **Week 1:** Create your account and one signup form. Put it at the end of every article and on the About page.
2. **Week 1:** Write a **3-email welcome sequence**: (1) welcome plus your best article, (2) your most useful tip, (3) what readers can expect from you.
3. **Week 2:** Make one simple **lead magnet** relevant to your niche, such as a checklist or a short template. Don't overthink it.
4. **Every 1–2 weeks:** Send a short email when you publish something new, with one useful takeaway in the email itself.
5. **Month 3:** Look at open and click rates. Keep doing what gets clicks.

Don't fill your emails with affiliate links. Recommend a tool only when it genuinely helps with what you're writing about, and disclose that the link is an affiliate link.

## Switching later is possible

Beginners often freeze over this decision. All three platforms let you export your subscribers. You'll have to rebuild forms and automations, which is a hassle, but a small list is easy to move. Pick the one that fits today and start collecting emails. An imperfect tool with 200 subscribers beats a perfect tool with zero.

## Things to check before signing up

- **Free plan terms:** subscriber limits, monthly send limits and whether automations are included.
- **Branding:** free plans often add the provider's logo to emails.
- **Sender requirements:** most providers require a custom domain email address (not a free webmail address) to send reliably. Plan for a domain-based address.
- **Legal basics:** include your physical mailing address (or a P.O. box) and an unsubscribe link in every marketing email, as required by laws such as CAN-SPAM in the US.

## Deliverability basics (whichever tool you pick)

Deliverability means your emails actually land in inboxes rather than spam. All three platforms work on it behind the scenes, but your own habits matter just as much:

- **Authenticate your domain.** Each platform walks you through adding DNS records (commonly SPF, DKIM and DMARC) so inbox providers trust your emails. It takes about 15 minutes and is worth doing on day one.
- **Never buy or import lists.** Only email people who opted in. Bought lists damage your sender reputation and break most providers' terms.
- **Use a confirmed (double) opt-in if you see many fake signups.** It makes your list a little smaller but much cleaner.
- **Send consistently.** A list you email once every six months forgets who you are, and complaints rise.
- **Clean your list.** Every few months, remove subscribers who haven't opened in a long time. Many tools can automate this.

## Common beginner mistakes

- **Waiting until you have traffic to start a list.** Set up the form now, and the first subscribers will come with the first visitors.
- **Over-designing emails.** Plain, personal emails often perform as well as heavily designed ones, and they're faster to write.
- **Only sending promotions.** Aim for most emails to be purely useful, and recommend products only when they're relevant.
- **Choosing a plan for the list you hope to have.** Choose for the list you have, and upgrade when you need to.

## When none of these is the right fit

If you run an online store, an ecommerce-focused email tool that connects deeply with your store platform may suit you better. And if you only want to send a few emails a year to friends or customers, a simple free plan from any of the three is plenty, so don't overbuy.

## Final verdict

- **Most new bloggers:** start with **MailerLite** for its simplicity and value.
- **Creators planning digital products:** choose **Kit**.
- **Marketers who want email plus webinars or funnels in one place:** choose **GetResponse**.

All three are legitimate, established tools. The best one is the one you set up this week.
