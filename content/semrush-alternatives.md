## What are you actually replacing?

Before you pick, be clear about which parts of Semrush you need. Most small sites use one or more of:

1. **Keyword research**: finding topics people search for.
2. **Rank tracking**: watching your positions over time.
3. **Site audit**: catching technical problems.
4. **Competitor research**: seeing what ranks for others.
5. **Backlink analysis**: seeing who links to whom.

No single cheap tool matches Semrush on all five. A smart budget stack often combines **one paid tool with two free ones**.

## 1. Google Search Console (free)

**What it is:** Google's own free tool for site owners. It shows the actual search queries your pages appear for, with impressions, clicks, average position and click-through rate. It also reports indexing problems.

**Why it belongs on this list:** For *your own site*, its data comes straight from Google rather than from estimates. Its "Performance" report is the best free keyword research tool most beginners ignore. Filter for queries where your average position is 8–20, and you'll find pages that a small improvement could push onto page one.

**What it won't replace:** competitor research, backlink research or keyword ideas for topics you haven't written about yet.

**Who it's for:** Everyone. Set it up on day one.

## 2. SE Ranking (budget all-rounder)

**What it is:** An SEO platform covering rank tracking, keyword research, site audit, competitor research and a backlink checker. It's often described as the closest budget-friendly match to an all-in-one suite.

**Strengths:**
- Rank tracking is a core strength, since the tool started as a rank tracker.
- A clean, approachable interface.
- Covers all five jobs above in one place.
- Entry plans have generally cost less than Semrush's.

**What it won't fully replace:** the sheer depth of Semrush's competitive databases, especially in smaller markets.

**Who it's for:** Solo site owners and small businesses who want one paid tool that does a bit of everything.

## 3. Mangools (simplest keyword research)

**What it is:** A bundle of five tools: KWFinder (keyword research), SERPChecker (search result analysis), SERPWatcher (rank tracking), LinkMiner (backlinks) and SiteProfiler (domain overview).

**Strengths:**
- KWFinder is known for being very beginner-friendly, especially for finding long-tail, lower-difficulty keywords.
- A friendly design that doesn't overwhelm you.
- Good for the "what should I write next?" question.

**What it won't fully replace:** an in-depth technical site audit. Mangools is focused on keywords and the search results rather than crawling your site.

**Who it's for:** Bloggers whose main bottleneck is choosing topics.

## 4. Ubersuggest (light, occasional use)

**What it is:** A keyword and site analysis tool with a limited free tier and paid plans.

**Strengths:**
- Easy to start, with some free daily searches.
- Gives keyword ideas, content ideas and a basic site audit.

**Weaknesses:** Free limits are tight, and its data is generally considered less deep than the bigger suites.

**Who it's for:** People who do keyword research a few times a month and don't want a subscription.

## 5. Moz Pro (established, link-focused)

**What it is:** One of the longest-running SEO software brands. It's known for its link metrics, including Domain Authority, which many people in the industry use as shorthand.

**Strengths:**
- Strong brand recognition and educational content (its free SEO guides are useful in their own right).
- Keyword research, rank tracking, site crawl and link research.

**Weaknesses:** Usually not the cheapest option, and some users find its keyword data smaller than the leading suites'.

**Who it's for:** People who care about link metrics or want an established brand. Moz also offers some free tools, such as its domain analysis lookups.

## 6. Bing Webmaster Tools (free)

**What it is:** Microsoft's equivalent of Search Console. Besides Bing search performance data, it has included free keyword research and site scan features.

**Why it's useful:** It's free, it gives a second dataset, and Bing traffic, though smaller, often converts well. You can import your site from Google Search Console to set it up quickly.

**Who it's for:** Everyone, as a free supplement.

## 7. Google Keyword Planner (free, with caveats)

**What it is:** A tool inside Google Ads, made for advertisers, that gives keyword ideas and search volume estimates.

**Caveats:** Without active ad spend, it often shows broad volume ranges rather than precise numbers. You need a Google Ads account, though you don't have to run ads.

**Who it's for:** Anyone who wants free keyword ideas straight from Google and doesn't mind approximate numbers.

## Our recommended budget stacks

**The $0 stack (months 0–3):**
- Google Search Console + Bing Webmaster Tools
- Google autocomplete, "People also ask" and Keyword Planner for ideas
- A spreadsheet to track 20–30 target keywords by hand each month

## How to choose in 15 minutes

1. Write down the **one job** you most need done (keyword ideas, rank tracking or audits).
2. Start free trials only for the tools that do that job well.
3. Test each on **your** site with the **same** keywords.
4. Keep the one you actually opened again without being reminded.
5. Set a calendar reminder to cancel the others before the trial ends.

## Mistakes to avoid

- **Paying for three overlapping tools.** Pick one paid tool.
- **Buying annual plans before you know you'll use them.** Start monthly, then switch to annual once the tool has proven itself (annual plans usually cost less per month).
- **Comparing metrics across tools.** Each tool's "difficulty" and "authority" scores use different formulas.
- **Ignoring Search Console** because it looks plain. It's the only one on this list with Google's own data about your site.

## FAQ

**Is there a completely free Semrush alternative?**
Not one that does everything. A combination of Google Search Console, Bing Webmaster Tools and Google Keyword Planner covers your own site's data and basic keyword ideas for free. What you give up is competitor and backlink depth.

**Can I rank without any paid SEO tool?**
Yes, especially early on. Many small sites grow on Search Console data and good topic choices alone. Paid tools mostly save time and help you prioritize once you have a lot of pages.

**Should I pick the tool with the biggest database?**
Not necessarily. For a small site in a major market, ease of use and price usually matter more than raw database size. You need enough good long-tail ideas, not all of them.

**How often should I check rankings?**
Weekly is plenty for a small site. Rankings move day to day, so look at trends over weeks rather than reacting to single days.

## Bottom line

For most small sites, the best Semrush alternative is **Google Search Console plus one budget tool**. If you want one paid tool that covers everything, start with **SE Ranking**. If keyword research is your main struggle, try **Mangools**. Upgrade to Semrush later, when your site's income justifies it.
