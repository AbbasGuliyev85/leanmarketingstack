You don't need a big budget to start a niche blog. You need a domain, somewhere to publish, a way to find topics, a way to measure results, and the patience to keep writing for months. Plenty of beginners overspend on tools before they've written ten articles.

This guide lays out a **lean, honest tool stack**, free wherever possible. For each item we explain why it's there and when, if ever, it's worth paying. We don't print prices, because they change. Always confirm current details on the vendor's own site before you buy.

## Step 1: Choose a narrow niche angle

Before tools, pick an angle narrow enough that a new site can compete. "Marketing" is too broad. "Budget SEO and email tools for solo bloggers" is specific enough to rank for long-tail searches like "Mangools vs SE Ranking" or "best free email tool for new bloggers".

A good beginner niche angle has:
- **Buyers:** people searching are close to making a purchase decision.
- **Specificity:** you can list 50 article ideas without straining.
- **Your edge:** you're willing to actually test or research the products.

## Step 2: Domain and publishing

**The $0 option:** a static site hosted free on **GitHub Pages** or **Cloudflare Pages**, on a free subdomain (for example `yourname.github.io` or `yourproject.pages.dev`). Static sites are plain HTML files, so they're fast, secure and cost nothing to host.

**The trade-off:** a free subdomain looks less professional, and moving to your own domain later means redirecting. Many people start free, then buy a domain once they're sure they'll keep going. A domain is usually the first, and smallest, paid upgrade worth making.

**When to consider WordPress hosting:** if you want plugins, a visual editor and lots of themes, managed WordPress hosting is the common route. It costs money every month or year, so it makes sense once you're committed.

## Step 3: Keyword research for $0

You can do solid keyword research without a paid tool:

1. **Google autocomplete:** type your topic and note the suggestions. Add letters ("best email tool for a…", "…b…") to get more.
2. **"People also ask":** the questions Google shows are real questions with real demand.
3. **Google Keyword Planner:** free inside a Google Ads account (you don't have to run ads). It shows volume ranges.
4. **Google Search Console:** once you publish, it shows the queries you're already appearing for. This is the most valuable free data you'll get.
5. **Reddit and forums:** search your niche plus "recommend" or "vs" to see how real people phrase their problems.

**When to pay:** once you have 20+ published pages and want to track rankings and find quick wins faster. ## Step 4: Measure what matters

- **Google Search Console** (free): impressions, clicks, average position, indexing problems. Check it weekly.
- **Bing Webmaster Tools** (free): a second view of your search data.
- **A privacy-friendly analytics tool:** Cloudflare Web Analytics is a free, privacy-focused option (check its current terms). If you use Google Analytics instead, update your privacy policy and consider cookie consent requirements in your readers' regions.

Early on, the only numbers that matter are **pages published** and **Search Console impressions**. Clicks and income come later.

## Step 6: Images and screenshots

Comparison and review content needs **original screenshots**. They prove you've actually looked at the product, and readers trust them more than stock photos. Use your operating system's built-in screenshot tool, and **Canva's free plan** for simple featured images.

Compress images before uploading (free tools like Squoosh work in the browser). Large images are the most common reason small sites load slowly.

## Step 7: Writing and editing

You don't need special writing software. Google Docs (or any editor) plus a free grammar checker is plenty. What matters is the process:

1. **Search the keyword** and read the top 5 results. Note what they miss.
2. **Outline** your article to answer the searcher's question better and more honestly.
3. **Write**, including real examples, screenshots and a clear verdict.
4. **Add an FTC disclosure** at the top of any post with affiliate links.
5. **Link internally** to 2–3 related posts on your site.

## Step 8 (later): Funnels, courses and digital products

Once you have an audience, you might sell a small digital product or course. An all-in-one platform like **Systeme.io** ({{AFFILIATE:systeme-io}}) bundles funnels, email and courses, and has offered a free plan (check current limits on its site). Don't set this up in month one. Come back to it when readers start asking you for more.

## What this stack costs

- **Months 0–3:** $0 is genuinely possible (static site, free subdomain, free tools).
- **First sensible upgrade:** your own domain, a small yearly cost.
- **Second upgrade (20+ pages):** one paid SEO tool, monthly at first.
- **Later:** paid email tier as your list grows. Hosting upgrades only if you outgrow static hosting.

## Realistic expectations

It would be dishonest to promise quick results. New sites commonly take **several months** to see meaningful search traffic, and early months often bring little or no income. What moves the needle:

- **Consistency:** publish on a schedule, such as 2–3 useful articles a week.
- **Specificity:** target long-tail, buyer-intent searches.
- **Honesty:** readers (and search engines) reward content that genuinely helps, including telling people when a free tool is enough.

## Tools we deliberately left out

Part of a lean stack is knowing what to skip early on:

- **Premium themes and page builders.** A clean, fast, readable layout beats a fancy one. Spend the time on content instead.
- **Several overlapping SEO tools.** One paid tool is enough, and zero is fine for the first few months.
- **Paid social media schedulers.** Until you know which channel works for you, post by hand.
- **Paid stock photo subscriptions.** Original screenshots are more useful for reviews and comparisons, and they're free.
- **Course platforms and funnel builders.** These only matter once you have something to sell and people asking to buy it.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is a free subdomain bad for SEO?**
It's not a penalty, but a custom domain is easier to brand and build trust with. If you plan to keep the site, buying a domain early saves you a migration later.

**Do I need WordPress to make money with affiliate links?**
No. Affiliate links work on any website, including a static site. WordPress is just one convenient publishing option.

**How many articles before I apply to affiliate programs?**
Some programs approve brand-new sites, and others want to see real content first. Having 5–10 quality articles published makes approval more likely in general.

## Your launch checklist

1. Pick a narrow niche angle and list 30 article ideas.
2. Set up a free static site with About, Disclosure and Privacy pages.
3. Verify the site in Google Search Console and Bing Webmaster Tools, and submit your sitemap.
4. Create a free email account and add a signup form.
5. Publish your first 10 articles over 3–4 weeks.
6. Check Search Console weekly, then improve pages that sit in positions 8–20.
7. Upgrade tools only when a free option clearly limits you.

The best tool stack is the cheapest one that lets you keep publishing.
