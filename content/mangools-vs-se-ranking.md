When people search for a "cheap Semrush alternative", two tools come up again and again: **Mangools** and **SE Ranking**. Both are popular with bloggers and small businesses, and both cost less than the big enterprise suites. They're built around different ideas, though, and picking the right one depends on what you need help with most.

This comparison is for beginners. We focus on the practical jobs you'll do every week and leave the spec sheets aside. Prices and plan limits change often, so we don't print them. Always confirm current details on the vendor's own site before you buy.

## The one-sentence difference

- **Mangools** is a **keyword research tool first**, with a few companion tools around it.
- **SE Ranking** is an **all-round SEO platform**, with rank tracking at its core and audits, competitor research and backlinks around it.

If your main question is *"What should I write about?"*, Mangools fits naturally. If your question is *"How is my whole site doing, and what should I fix?"*, SE Ranking fits better.

## What's in each tool

### Mangools

Mangools bundles five tools under one subscription:

- **KWFinder:** keyword research, focused on finding long-tail keywords with lower difficulty.
- **SERPChecker:** analyzes the current top search results for a keyword.
- **SERPWatcher:** rank tracking.
- **LinkMiner:** backlink research.
- **SiteProfiler:** a domain overview for any website.

The bundle is known for its clean, friendly design. Many beginners find KWFinder one of the easiest keyword tools to understand.

### SE Ranking

SE Ranking covers the main jobs of an SEO platform:

- **Rank tracking:** its original core feature.
- **Keyword research:** ideas, volumes and difficulty.
- **Website audit:** crawls your site for technical issues.
- **Competitor research:** see which keywords other sites rank for.
- **Backlink checker:** research and monitor links.

It also offers extras such as content and on-page tools and reporting features aimed at agencies.

## Head-to-head on the jobs that matter

### 1. Finding keywords you can actually rank for

For a new site, this is the job that matters most. Both tools give you keyword ideas, search volume estimates and a difficulty score.

**Mangools' edge:** KWFinder was built around this job. Its workflow (type a seed keyword, scan the suggestions, check the search results) is quick and easy to follow. For someone who has never used an SEO tool, it's a gentle introduction.

**SE Ranking's edge:** its keyword research sits next to rank tracking and competitor research, so you can go straight from "what does my competitor rank for?" to "which of those can I target?"

**Verdict:** Slight edge to **Mangools** for pure, beginner-friendly keyword discovery.

### 2. Tracking your rankings

**SE Ranking** has rank tracking at its core, and it's a common reason people choose it. **Mangools** includes SERPWatcher, which covers basic tracking well. Check how many tracked keywords each plan includes, since that's often the limit you hit first.

**Verdict:** Edge to **SE Ranking**, especially if you'll track hundreds of keywords or several sites.

### 3. Technical site audits

**SE Ranking** includes a website audit that crawls your pages and flags issues such as broken links, missing meta tags, slow pages and indexing problems. **Mangools** focuses on keywords and search results and doesn't center on site crawling.

**Verdict:** Clear edge to **SE Ranking**. If you choose Mangools, you can cover the basics with free tools: Google Search Console's indexing reports and PageSpeed Insights.

### 4. Competitor research

Both tools let you look at a competitor's domain. SE Ranking's competitor research is more of a dedicated module. Mangools' SiteProfiler gives a quick overview.

**Verdict:** Edge to **SE Ranking** for depth. Mangools is enough for a quick look.

### 5. Ease of use

Both are considered easier than the big enterprise suites. Mangools is often praised as one of the most beginner-friendly SEO tools around. SE Ranking is also approachable but has more sections, simply because it does more.

**Verdict:** Slight edge to **Mangools**.

## How to test both fairly

Both tools have historically offered free trials. Here's a fair one-week test:

1. **Pick 3 seed keywords** from your niche.
2. In each tool, find **10 long-tail keywords** you believe you could rank for. Note how long it took and how confident you feel about the list.
3. Add your site and track the **same 20 keywords** in both.
4. If testing SE Ranking, run a **site audit** and see whether the top issues make sense to you.
5. At the end of the week, ask yourself: *which one did I open without being told to?*

Set calendar reminders to cancel whichever you don't keep.

## Don't forget the free layer

Whichever you choose, keep these free tools running:

- **Google Search Console** for your real queries, clicks and indexing problems.
- **Bing Webmaster Tools** for a second view of your search data.
- **PageSpeed Insights** for page speed checks.

A paid tool adds a layer on top of these. It doesn't replace them.

## Common questions

**Can I use both?**
You can, but most small sites don't need to. Start with one, and only add a second if you hit a clear limit.

**Are the keyword volumes accurate?**
All third-party tools estimate. Treat volumes as a rough sense of size, not exact counts. Your Search Console data shows real impressions once you rank.

**Should I buy annual?**
Not until you've used a tool for at least a month. Annual billing usually lowers the monthly cost, but only if you keep using it.

**Which is better for a local business?**
Usually SE Ranking, because the audit and rank tracking help with the technical and local visibility questions small businesses tend to have.

**Is a cheap tool enough to compete with big sites?**
Tool choice is rarely what holds a small site back. Picking specific long-tail topics and writing better answers than what currently ranks matters far more than which dashboard you use.

## A sample weekly workflow with each tool

To make the difference concrete, here's how a typical week might look for a blogger using each tool.

**With Mangools:**
- **Monday:** Open KWFinder, enter a seed topic from your niche, and save 5–10 long-tail keywords with low difficulty to a list.
- **Tuesday:** Check each keyword in SERPChecker. Are small blogs or forums ranking? If yes, it's a good target.
- **Wednesday to Friday:** Write one article targeting the best keyword.
- **Friday:** Glance at SERPWatcher to see how last month's articles are moving.

**With SE Ranking:**
- **Monday:** Check the rank tracking dashboard for pages that moved into positions 8–20, your quick-win candidates.
- **Tuesday:** Improve one quick-win page (better title, an added section, internal links).
- **Wednesday:** Use keyword research or competitor research to pick the next new topic.
- **Thursday to Friday:** Write, then run a site audit once a month and fix only the important issues.

Neither workflow is better in the abstract. Mangools suits a "publish new content" rhythm. SE Ranking suits a "grow and maintain the whole site" rhythm.
