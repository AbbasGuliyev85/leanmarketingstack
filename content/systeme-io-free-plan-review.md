"Free plan" is one of the most stretched phrases in software marketing. Sometimes it means a 14-day trial with a credit card on file. Sometimes it means a plan so limited you can't do anything useful. And sometimes it really is free for as long as you want it.

**Systeme.io** ({{AFFILIATE:systeme-io}}) is an all-in-one platform for funnels, email marketing, online courses, websites and blogs, and it advertises a free plan. In this review we answer one question: **is it actually free, and is the free plan useful enough to run a small online business on?**

**How we checked:** every number in this article comes from the official Systeme.io pricing page at systeme.io/pricing, **checked on October 22, 2026**. That page said it was last updated in April 2026. We haven't run a paid account, so we don't make claims about speed, deliverability or support quality. Pricing changes, so confirm current details on the vendor's site before you decide.

## The short answer

Yes. According to Systeme.io's own pricing page, the free plan **never expires and doesn't require a credit card**. It isn't a trial. You can stay on it for as long as your business fits inside its limits.

The real question isn't whether it's free. It's **how long you'll fit inside the limits**, and what happens when you don't.

## Is there a catch?

There are no hidden costs to *stay* on the free plan. There are three real limits to understand before you build anything on it.

### 1. The 2,000-contact ceiling

Systeme.io's plans are organized mainly around **contacts**. Once your list grows past 2,000 contacts, you'll need the paid Startup plan. For a brand-new creator, 2,000 contacts is a lot of room. Plenty of small newsletters and course businesses never reach it in their first year. If you're moving over an existing list that's already bigger than that, the free plan isn't for you.

### 2. One automation rule and one email campaign

This is the limit most likely to pinch first. Unlimited email *sends* sounds generous, and it is. But the free plan lists only **1 automation rule** and **1 email campaign**. In practice you can run a simple setup: one welcome sequence and regular newsletters. If you want several sequences that branch based on what people do, you'll run into this cap before you hit the contact limit.

### 3. Three funnels and one course

Three sales funnels is enough for a lead magnet funnel, a sales page for one product, and maybe one webinar or upsell experiment. One course with up to 500 students fits a first product well. If you plan to sell several courses, you'll need a paid plan.

## Who the free plan is actually good for

Based on the published limits, the free plan suits:

- **New creators and bloggers** who want to build an email list and capture leads without paying for a separate email tool.
- **People launching a first digital product** (one course, one ebook, one coaching offer) who want sales pages, checkout and delivery in one place.
- **Anyone testing an idea** who doesn't want to commit to a monthly bill before they've made a sale.
- **People who want their own affiliate program**: the free plan includes one, which is unusual at that price point.

It's a poor fit if you:

- Already have more than 2,000 contacts.
- Need several complex automations from day one.
- Plan to run many products, courses or funnels at once.

Systeme.io's own guidance on the pricing page is simple. Under 2,000 contacts, use the free plan. From 2,000 to 5,000, use Startup. From 5,000 to 10,000, use Webinar. Above 10,000, use Unlimited.

### Upgrading and cancelling

According to the pricing page, you can upgrade or cancel at any time from your account settings. If you cancel, you keep your plan until the next billing date and then your account **reverts to the free plan**. Your account doesn't just disappear. That's a useful safety net: if a launch doesn't work out, you can drop back to $0 instead of losing everything.

## How it compares with stitching tools together

The main pitch for an all-in-one tool is that it replaces several subscriptions. Systeme.io's pricing page makes the comparison itself: it lists ClickFunnels at $97/month, Mailchimp at $20/month, Teachable at $39/month and Tapfiliate at $89/month, for $245/month combined. That's the vendor's own comparison, so treat it as marketing. Competitors' prices and plans change, and you may not need all four tools.

The basic point still holds for a beginner. If you need **a landing page, an email list, a checkout and somewhere to host a course**, a free plan that covers all four is a lot simpler than four separate accounts and the integrations between them.

## Common questions

### Is the Systeme.io free plan a trial?

No. The official pricing page says the free plan never expires and doesn't need a credit card.

### Does Systeme.io take a cut of my sales?

The pricing page lists 0% transaction fees on all plans, including free. Stripe or PayPal will still charge their usual processing fees.

### What happens if I go over 2,000 contacts?

You'll need to move to a paid plan. The Startup plan was listed at $17/month for up to 5,000 contacts when we checked.

### Can I go back to free after upgrading?

According to the pricing page, cancelling a paid plan returns your account to the free plan at the next billing date.

### Is it good for bloggers?

The free plan includes one blog, email marketing and funnels, so it can work as a starting point. Many bloggers prefer a dedicated blogging platform for the site itself and use Systeme.io only for the email list and products. Either approach works.

## The verdict

**Yes, the Systeme.io free plan is actually free.** There's no trial clock and no credit card, according to the official pricing page. The limits are real but reasonable for a beginner: 2,000 contacts, 3 funnels, 1 course and, the tightest one, a single automation rule.

If you're starting from zero and want to build a list and sell a first product without a monthly bill, it's one of the easiest ways to begin. When you outgrow it, the first paid step is $17/month, which is modest compared with many funnel tools.
