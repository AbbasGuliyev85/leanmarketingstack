If you're about to sell your first online course, two names come up again and again: **Kajabi** and **Systeme.io** ({{AFFILIATE:systeme-io}}). Both call themselves all-in-one platforms. Both host courses, send email, build sales pages and take payments. But their prices are in completely different leagues, and for a beginner that difference matters more than almost any feature.

This article answers one practical question: **how much will each platform cost you in your first year of selling a course, and is the expensive one worth it?**

**How we checked:** every price and limit below comes from the official pricing pages at kajabi.com/pricing and systeme.io/pricing, both **checked on October 27, 2026**. The Systeme.io page said it was last updated in April 2026. Card fees for outside processors come from Stripe's official pricing page, checked the same day. We haven't run paid accounts on either platform, so we don't make claims about speed, support quality or email deliverability. Prices change, so confirm them on each vendor's site before you buy.

## The short answer

- **Systeme.io** has a free plan that never expires and needs no credit card, according to its pricing page. Paid plans run from $17 to $97 a month.
- **Kajabi** has no free plan. It offers a free trial, and its cheapest plan, Basic, costs **$179 a month**, or **$143 a month billed annually**.
- For someone who hasn't sold a course yet, Systeme.io can cost **$0 in year one**. Kajabi Basic costs **$1,716 to $2,148 in year one**, depending on billing.
- Kajabi gives you more room on its entry plan (unlimited funnels and landing pages, 5 products) and extras like AI credits on higher plans and a branded mobile app. Whether that's worth about $1,700 or more a year depends on whether you already have sales.

## Year-one cost for a first course

Let's put real numbers on the scenario most beginners are in: one course, a small email list, and no sales yet.

| Scenario | Systeme.io | Kajabi |
|---|---|---|
| Under 2,000 contacts, cheapest plan | $0 (Free) | $1,716 (Basic, annual) or $2,148 (Basic, monthly) |
| 2,000 to 5,000 contacts | $204 (Startup, monthly) | Basic covers up to 2,500; past that, the next plan is Growth at $2,388 annual or $2,988 monthly |
| Need several courses and big funnels | $1,164 (Unlimited, monthly) | $1,716 to $2,148 (Basic, if 5 products is enough) |

A few things stand out:

1. **Even Systeme.io's top plan costs less than Kajabi's bottom plan.** Unlimited at $97 a month comes to $1,164 a year on monthly billing. Kajabi Basic is $1,716 a year at its cheapest.
2. **The contact limits are close at the low end.** Systeme.io Free allows 2,000 contacts and Kajabi Basic allows 2,500. The real gap is in what you pay for them.
3. **Kajabi's jump after Basic is steep.** Going from 2,500 to more contacts on Kajabi means Growth at $199 to $249 a month, unless you buy contact add-ons. On Systeme.io, the step from 2,000 to 5,000 contacts is $17 a month.

Over **three years** on the cheapest plans, the gap gets bigger: $0 on Systeme.io Free versus $5,148 on Kajabi Basic with annual billing ($1,716 times 3). That's simple multiplication from today's listed prices; real prices may change during that time.

## Payment fees: the cost people forget

Neither platform takes a percentage of your sales as a platform fee. But you still pay card processing, and the way each one handles it is different.

**On Systeme.io**, you connect Stripe or PayPal and Systeme.io adds nothing on top, according to its pricing page. Stripe's standard US rate, from Stripe's own pricing page, is **2.9% + 30¢ per successful domestic card transaction**.

**On Kajabi**, the pricing page lists these rates for its own processor, Kajabi Payments, for US businesses:

| Kajabi plan | Kajabi Payments card fee (US) | Extra fee if you use a third-party processor instead |
|---|---|---|
| Basic | 2.9% + $0.30 | 2% |
| Growth | 2.8% + $0.30 | 1% |
| Pro | 2.7% + $0.30 | 0.5% |

The page notes that the third-party fee excludes PayPal and Kajabi Payments, applies in regions where Kajabi Payments is offered, and comes on top of whatever the outside provider charges. So if you're on Basic and you'd rather use your own Stripe account, you'd pay Stripe's fee plus Kajabi's 2%.

Here's what that looks like for a simple example: **100 sales of a $100 course ($10,000 in revenue)**, all US domestic cards.

| Setup | Card fees | Platform's extra cut | Total fees |
|---|---|---|---|
| Systeme.io + Stripe | $320 (2.9% + 30¢ × 100) | $0 | $320 |
| Kajabi Basic + Kajabi Payments | $320 (2.9% + $0.30 × 100) | $0 | $320 |
| Kajabi Basic + your own Stripe | $320 | $200 (2% of $10,000) | $520 |

This is our arithmetic using listed rates, not a quote. The takeaway: if you stick with Kajabi Payments, fees at this size are about the same as Stripe on Systeme.io. The real difference is still the monthly subscription.

## What Kajabi gives you for the extra money

To be fair to Kajabi, the price isn't just a markup. According to its pricing page:

- **Unlimited funnels and landing pages** on every plan, while Systeme.io Free caps you at 3 funnels and Startup at 10.
- **Up to 5 products on Basic**, compared with 1 course on Systeme.io Free. (Kajabi counts "products" broadly; its product menu includes courses, communities, coaching, memberships, podcasts and digital downloads.)
- **AI credits** on higher plans: 0 on Basic, 500 on Growth and 1,000 on Pro, with extra credits available to buy.
- **A branded mobile app**, which the page lists as included and values at $199 a month, and an **API** valued at $25 a month. Check the comparison table for which plans get each.
- **Admin seats** for a team: 2 on Basic, 11 on Growth, 26 on Pro.

Kajabi's own FAQ puts it plainly: if you only want a course platform, it admits Kajabi is expensive, and it argues the value comes from replacing your email tool, website, community and checkout once you're past a few thousand contacts. Its FAQ also says Basic is the right starting plan "if you haven't sold anything yet."

## What Systeme.io gives you for less

Systeme.io's pricing page lists a broad toolset even on the free plan: sales funnels, email marketing, a blog, an online course, your own affiliate program, a custom domain and checkout through Stripe or PayPal. Its limits on the free plan are real, though:

- **1 automation rule and 1 email campaign**, which is the limit most likely to pinch first.
- **3 funnels and 1 course.**
- **2,000 contacts.**

One detail to watch: the free plan FAQ lists **500 students**, while another section of the same pricing page says all plans include unlimited students. We're reporting both as written. If student count matters to you, confirm it inside the account before you launch.

When you outgrow Free, the paid steps are small: $17, then $47, then $97 a month. And according to the pricing page, if you cancel a paid plan, your account **reverts to the free plan** at the next billing date instead of being shut down.

## Who should pick which

**Pick Systeme.io if:**

- You haven't made your first course sale yet.
- You want to test an idea without a monthly bill.
- Your list is under 2,000 to 5,000 contacts.
- You want your own affiliate program without paying extra for it.

**Consider Kajabi if:**

- You already have steady course revenue and $143 to $179 a month is a small share of it.
- You need many funnels and landing pages from day one.
- You want a branded mobile app or a larger team account.
- You'd rather pay more for an established, polished platform and can use the 30-day money-back guarantee to test it.

There's no shame in starting cheap. Many creators start on a low-cost tool, prove that people will buy, and then decide whether an upgrade is worth it. ## Common questions

### Does Kajabi have a free plan?

No. When we checked, Kajabi's pricing page offered a free trial and three paid plans starting at $143 a month billed annually or $179 a month billed monthly. Systeme.io does have a free plan that, according to its pricing page, never expires.

### Is Systeme.io cheaper than Kajabi?

Yes, at every level we compared. Systeme.io's most expensive plan ($97 a month) costs less than Kajabi's cheapest plan ($143 to $179 a month).

### Does either platform take a cut of my sales?

Neither charges a revenue share, according to their pricing pages. You still pay card processing fees. On Kajabi, using a third-party processor instead of Kajabi Payments adds an extra 0.5% to 2% depending on your plan, where Kajabi Payments is available.

### Can I move from Systeme.io to Kajabi later?

Your course content, videos and email list belong to you, so moving is possible, though it takes work. Starting cheap and moving once you have proof of demand is a common path.

### Can I get a refund from Kajabi if it isn't right for me?

Kajabi's pricing page says every plan has a 30-day money-back guarantee. Read the terms at signup.

## The verdict

For a **first online course**, Systeme.io is the more sensible starting point for most beginners: it can cost $0 while you build and test, and its paid steps are small. **Kajabi** is a polished, bigger platform with more room on its entry plan, but at $1,716 to $2,148 a year for Basic, it makes the most sense once your course is already earning.
